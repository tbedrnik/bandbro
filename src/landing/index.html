The songbook

Everything the band plays, in one place everyone can reach.

Browse a curated library, your band's shelf, or your own. Fork any song into your own scope and change whatever you like — the original stays untouched.

Curated, band and personal shelves

Three scopes, one browser. Songs you add to a band are shared with the band; your own stay yours. Search by title, artist, key or tag.

Fork, don't fight

Your guitarist wants a different intro? Fork the chart into your band's shelf. BandBro keeps the provenance so you can always see where it came from.

A ChordPro editor with a live preview

Write chords over lyrics in plain ChordPro on the left, watch the finished sheet render on the right. Transpose the source itself when a song is written in the wrong key for everyone.

Import a sheet in one paste

Paste an akordy.kytary.cz link and BandBro converts the page into a proper ChordPro chart — sections, inline chords and all — ready to tidy up.

H or B — your notation

Chords are stored the international way and shown the way Central Europe reads them: H for B natural, B for B flat. Nobody has to translate in their head at rehearsal.

Bands, roles and invite links

Admins run the band, writers edit the songs, readers play them. Invite a bandmate with a link or hold up a QR code at rehearsal — no email round trip.

One chart, two views

The capo argument, settled.

Everyone reads what they play.

A song is written once, with the shapes the guitarist fingers and a {capo} line. The keys player flips one toggle and sees the same song at concert pitch. Nobody rewrites anything.

  • As-fingered ↔ concert pitch on a single switch.
  • Transpose up or down for the singer who's fighting the top note.
  • Slash chords and odd spellings survive the trip.
  • Pick a default view once in preferences and forget about it.

Huge chords, no chrome, no fumbling.

Dark stage palette, chords sized to be read at arm's length, and the controls you touch mid-song within thumb reach. Everything else lives one swipe away in a drawer.

  • Auto-scroll with a speed you set, prev/next by tap.
  • Fit-to-screen sizes the song to your tablet, or lay it out in two or three columns.
  • Jump to any song in the set — search by title, artist or a line of the lyric.
  • Text size, spacing and theme are per device — your phone and the drummer's iPad each keep their own.
Setlists

Build the set, print the set, play the set.

Drag songs into order minutes before you go on. Export the whole thing as a proper chord-sheet PDF — one song per page, no page turns mid-chorus, a table of contents at the front, and capo'd songs printed as fingered, at concert pitch, or both.

Drag to reorder

Grip a row and move it — with a finger, a mouse or the keyboard. The order saves itself.

PDF that reads like a chart

Rendered server-side by the reference ChordPro engine: two columns when a song needs them, chords in red, nothing spilling over a page break.

Download for the gig

One tap puts the whole setlist on your device, chord sheets and all, ready for a basement with no bars of signal.

Offline

The venue's wifi is not your problem.

BandBro installs to your home screen and opens signed in with no connection at all. Downloaded setlists play from the device, and the offline shelf searches every one of them — titles, artists and lyrics, accents folded so “sen” still finds “Šeň”.

  • Installs as a real app on iOS and Android.
  • Live mode runs from the downloaded copy, no requests, no stalls.
  • Controls that need the server get out of the way instead of teasing you with a greyed-out button.

Your audience

Put the room in the song.

Share a five-character code or a QR from the stage. Anyone can open a read-only live view — no account, no install — and it follows you from song to song. They pick their own text size and theme; nothing they do reaches your screen.
